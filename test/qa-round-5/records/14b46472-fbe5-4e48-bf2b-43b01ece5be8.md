## What is broken
- The architecture requires FocusBroker to track active surfaces, restore focus after modals, reserve app-level shortcuts, and provide recovery, but no design document defines the subsystem promised for Phase 7. docs/02-architecture.md:164
- Focus is currently split across app activation, tile mouse handling, terminal first-responder behavior, and browser first-responder behavior instead of one authority. Sources/ContinuumRevived/App/ContinuumApp.swift:552 Sources/ContinuumRevived/Canvas/TileNSView.swift:75 Sources/ContinuumRevived/TerminalEngine/GhosttyTerminalView.swift:17 Sources/ContinuumRevived/BrowserEngine/WKWebViewBrowserRuntime.swift:119

## Why
- `applicationDidBecomeActive` directly walks runtime arrays and falls back to the last terminal runtime, so activation policy lives outside a FocusBroker. Sources/ContinuumRevived/App/ContinuumApp.swift:552
- `TileNSView.mouseDown` only brings the tile forward before drag or content handling, so click focus is not coordinated with a central broker. Sources/ContinuumRevived/Canvas/TileNSView.swift:75
- Terminal and browser surfaces expose local first-responder behavior without a shared reserved-shortcut policy. Sources/ContinuumRevived/TerminalEngine/GhosttyTerminalView.swift:45 Sources/ContinuumRevived/BrowserEngine/WKWebViewBrowserRuntime.swift:119
